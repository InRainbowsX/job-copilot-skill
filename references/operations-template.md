# Operations Template

Apply this template when the candidate targets internet operations roles, including growth operations, user operations, activity operations, content operations, and self-media operations.

When the materials are clearly closer to `内容运营 / 自媒体运营 / 个人内容项目`, also consult [content-operations-knowledge-base.md](./content-operations-knowledge-base.md) before choosing packaging angles or review standards.
When those cases need true content-operations or self-media standards rather than broad operations handling, also apply [content-operations-template.md](./content-operations-template.md).
When the resume includes `个人创作期 / 个人账号 / 内容项目`, do not stop at generic gap handling. Only escalate into the content-operations template when the candidate's target role is clearly `内容运营 / 自媒体运营`, or when content and self-media signals are the `dominant content evidence` inside the broader operations profile. Otherwise, keep the main route in broad operations and only borrow the content-project packaging boundary as needed.

## Role Definition

This family is judged on whether the candidate can connect business goals to operating actions, explain the funnel or audience they worked on, and show iteration logic through metrics and review.

Strong operations candidates usually show:

- a clear target user or audience
- a traffic, engagement, retention, or conversion goal
- concrete operating actions instead of generic execution wording
- metric-based iteration rather than one-off delivery
- repeatable playbooks or reusable lessons

## Routing Signals

Prefer this family when the materials emphasize:

- user growth, retention, activation, funnel conversion
- campaign planning, community management, channel operations
- content strategy, topic selection, distribution, self-media account growth
- activity review, lead generation, or user behavior analysis

Common keywords:

- DAU, retention, conversion, funnel, traffic, users, reach, engagement
- activity, campaign, growth, community, content calendar, self-media
- followers, reads, CTR, GMV, registrations, activation, private traffic

For content-heavy cases, do not stop at generic operations routing. Check whether the stronger language should shift toward:

- audience positioning
- topic selection
- content pillars
- editorial cadence
- platform-native distribution
- review and iteration loops

## Agent 1 Deep-Dive Prompts

Always ask enough questions to pin down target, action, and measurable result.

### Shared Operations Questions

- What was the core goal: growth, retention, conversion, activation, or content output?
- Who was the target audience or user segment?
- What operating actions did you design versus only execute?
- What channels or surfaces were used?
- What was the funnel from exposure to final outcome?
- Which metrics did you track weekly or per campaign?
- What changed after the first attempt? What did you iterate?
- What was your personal ownership boundary versus the team or design team?

### Self-Media Add-On Questions

- What was the account positioning and target audience?
- How did you choose topics or content themes?
- What was the production cadence and review loop?
- Which content performed best, and why?
- Did the account drive downstream conversion, leads, or brand lift?
- Which results were account-level and which were your direct contribution?

## Packaging Rules

Emphasize:

- the goal and audience first
- the operating action or strategy second
- the metric result or learnings third

Good operations wording usually follows:

`goal -> action -> iteration -> result`

Preferred packaging angles:

- growth logic
- funnel optimization
- content strategy
- campaign planning and review
- user insight turned into action

Safe upgrade moves:

- turn generic execution into explicit action ownership when support exists
- connect scattered tasks into one operating loop
- convert vanity descriptions into business or funnel language
- highlight iteration decisions, not just end results

High-risk moves:

- claiming full strategy ownership when the candidate mainly executed
- inflating account-level or campaign-level results into personal results
- presenting natural traffic growth as intentional operating success without support
- writing only vanity metrics with no downstream meaning

## Agent 2 Review Focus

In addition to the shared rubric, check:

- whether the candidate can explain the target audience and operating goal
- whether metrics have a clear definition or observation window
- whether strategy ownership is real or just execution retold as strategy
- whether content or activity results can be tied to actual actions
- whether self-media results are distinguishable from platform luck or team contribution

Downgrade when:

- the candidate cannot explain why a campaign or content direction was chosen
- the candidate reports growth numbers but cannot explain the funnel
- ownership over strategy, review, or iteration is unsupported

## Mock Interview Themes

Use operations-specific pressure tests such as:

- Why did you choose this target audience or content direction?
- Which metric mattered most, and why?
- If the first campaign underperformed, how did you iterate?
- What part did you personally own?
- Which result can truly be attributed to your action?
- For self-media operations, what made a topic likely to work?
